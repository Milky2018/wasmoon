## Description

Rematerialization needs to know what rebuilding a value actually costs.
Without that, the choice between rebuilding and spilling is a guess.

Measured cost of materializing a constant on AArch64, against a reload
from a spill slot at one instruction:

| value | instructions |
| --- | ---: |
| 5, 1020, 65535, 65536 | 1 |
| 4294967295, 305419896 | 2 |
| 81985529216486895 | 4 |
| `f64.const 3.14159` | 5 |

At one instruction, rebuilding matches the reload and additionally saves
the spill store, the stack slot, and the cross-block live range, so it
strictly wins. At four or five it is several times the reload and is
likely a loss. The interesting middle is two instructions, which should
be measured rather than assumed.

This is the piece Cranelift structurally lacks: its remat rules live in
the target-independent mid-end, so they cannot consult a cost, and the
result is a blanket rule over every `iconst`, `f32const` and `f64const`
regardless of what materializing one costs on the target. See ISS-403.

## Design

Keep the policy private to MilkIR. The first cut accepts operand-free integer
constants with at most one non-zero 16-bit lane, matching one `movz` in the
current AArch64 emitter and one materialization on x64. Zero is also accepted.

Exclude float, vector, ALU, and recursive recipes. Do not add a target callback
until two real target policies differ; one conservative policy needs no seam.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [x] A predicate identifies single-`movz`-form integer constants as
      cheap to rebuild.
- [x] Float and vector constants are excluded.
- [x] The predicate stays package-private and requires no target dependency or
      optimizer configuration.
- [x] Unit tests pin the boundary cases, including 65536 as cheap and
      4294967295 as not.
