## Description

AEGIS-128L spends 62,377 us in register allocation. Function 56 alone spends
26,269 us, including 14,709 us in bundle allocation. Its deterministic loop
performs 10,526 register probes and 603,391 occupied-segment visits but only
4,620 newly observed conflicts, 52 evictions, and 64 splits. This is sustained
hot-path traversal, not runaway queue or backtracking churn.

The retained ISS-371 change kept reference-counted `AllocationSegment` records
out of scan-only comparisons without changing decisions or visit counts. That
single representation change reduced aggregate bundle time by about 11
percent, directly demonstrating that object and reference-count traffic is a
material remaining cost.

## Design

Introduce a serial-compilation-owned allocation context that is reset for each
function and owns dense primitive arrays for hot range, segment, bundle, owner,
endpoint, mark, and allocation facts. Use stable integer IDs in probe and queue
paths. Reuse capacities for conflict sets, work queues, mark arrays, temporary
range lists, and spillset pools across functions compiled by one serial module
job.

Use selective value representation only for small immutable facts after
checking copy and ABI semantics. Do not mechanically mark mutable
owner-bearing `AllocationSegment` or `AllocationBundle` records as value types;
prefer dense index or structure-of-arrays storage for them.

Preserve the current merged traversal and intrusive occupancy index unless a
measured replacement wins in wall time. Prior binary, galloping, and more
aggressive reseek experiments reduced visits but increased total compilation
time.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] Register probes and allocation queues use dense IDs and primitive indexed facts in their common paths without materializing reference-counted segment or bundle records.
- [ ] Scratch collections and temporary storage are cleared and reused, including across functions in one serial module compilation.
- [ ] Allocation decisions, deterministic loop counters, spills, reloads, moves, and generated code remain unchanged unless an explicitly documented code-quality improvement is separately accepted.
- [ ] Across at least seven cache-isolated AEGIS-128L captures, aggregate bundle allocation has a median at or below 15 ms and function 56 complete register allocation has a median at or below 15 ms.
- [ ] Function 56 remains at the 16,036-byte control, and bounded BLAKE2b and Salsa20 controls do not regress materially; no full algorithms corpus is required for the performance comparison.
- [ ] Regalloc, MachV adapter, AArch64, x64, strict allocation-validation, formatting, warning-73, generated-interface, and module-boundary gates pass.

## Notes
- 2026-08-28: Wasmtime/regalloc2 is useful here as a data-layout reference: it centralizes dense vectors, cached conflict state, reusable scratch collections, and bump-backed small lists. This issue does not require copying its allocation policy.
- 2026-08-28: Work started after ISS-443. The current seven-run AEGIS median
  is 51,725 us for aggregate regalloc, 25,328 us for bundle allocation, and
  22,088 us for function 56 regalloc. The first bounded slice removes the
  queue's retained `Array[AllocationBundle]`: immutable priority and weight
  facts are captured in dense queue entries at enqueue time, while mutable
  bundle ownership remains in the allocator context.
- 2026-08-28: The queue slice is retained as a small measured win and ownership
  cleanup, not presented as the main ISS-444 result. Queue comparisons no longer
  dereference reference-counted bundles. Seven cache-isolated AEGIS runs reduce
  median bundle allocation from 25,328 to 25,214 us and aggregate regalloc from
  51,725 to 51,502 us. All loop counters and code-size vectors are unchanged.
- 2026-08-28: A direct integer cursor in the occupancy scan was rejected after
  a paired build comparison. Despite removing the one-element `Array[Int]` and
  `Int?` facade, it increased median AEGIS module compilation from 119,696 to
  125,668 us and function 56 regalloc from 22,341 to 23,742 us. The experiment
  was fully reverted rather than retaining a locally simpler but slower path.
- 2026-08-28: A temporary classification probe found that 254,556 of function
  56's 259,364 overlapping occupied-segment visits immediately repeated the
  same bundle owner. The occupancy index now maintains the number of contiguous
  runs for each allocated owner in O(1) on insertion and removal. Once a probe
  records a conflicting owner whose segments form one run, it jumps directly
  past that run. Seven cache-isolated AEGIS captures reduce occupied visits from
  603,391 to 97,020 while preserving 1,679 queue pops, 10,526 register probes,
  4,620 conflicts, 52 evictions, 64 splits, 65 second-chance attempts, every
  spill/reload/move vector, the 16,036-byte function 56, and 59,428 total bytes.
  Against the paired HEAD medians, aggregate bundle allocation falls from
  25,223 to 21,554 us (-14.5%), aggregate regalloc from 51,690 to 47,904 us
  (-7.3%), function 56 bundle allocation from 12,668 to 9,879 us (-22.0%), and
  function 56 regalloc from 22,341 to 19,385 us (-13.2%). Raw retained captures
  are in `target/aegis-iss444-owner-run.UgCzPk`; the paired control was built
  from `97d00caf`, the immediately preceding `dev` commit.
