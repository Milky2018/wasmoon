## Description

After ISS-229 establishes production bundle identity, the allocator must make register, eviction, and split decisions for the bundle rather than independently for each fragment. Merely assigning related segments the same soft preference does not guarantee coalescing and can still produce edge moves or inconsistent eviction decisions.

## Design

Queue bundles by aggregate live extent, while keeping aggregate local spill cost as the separate eviction decision. Probe a candidate register against every fragment in the bundle, including fixed constraints and clobber points. Allocate the whole bundle when possible; otherwise compare aggregate eviction cost and split at a concrete conflict boundary before spilling. Requeue evicted or split bundle pieces deterministically. Preserve the existing per-operand location/edit result contract and independent symbolic allocation verification.

Reuse the repository's existing backtracking conflict, eviction, and split policies where they match the production `ProgramRange` model. Do not retain two independently evolving allocation loops after the cutover.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [x] A conflict-free bundle receives one register across all of its fragments and produces no transfer between coalesced SSA values.
- [x] Bundle conflicts are costed across all fragments; eviction cannot partially overwrite an allocated bundle.
- [x] A conflicting bundle is split at a validated boundary and its pieces are requeued without losing fixed constraints or use weights.
- [x] Calls/clobbers, loops, critical edges, multiple predecessors, and disjoint live-range fragments have focused allocation-plan snapshots.
- [x] The generic and materialized-VCode allocation verifiers accept every new regression plan and reject deliberately corrupted plans.
- [x] The complete native test suite and recursive JIT WAST corpus pass.

## Notes
- 2026-07-22: The current `preferred_by_value` and `edge_target_registers` heuristics influence probe order but do not provide bundle atomicity, transitive coalescing, or bundle-level splitting.
- 2026-07-22: The production allocator now queues bundles by aggregate live extent, probes every fragment, costs unique conflicting bundles by local spill weight, removes and requeues evicted bundles atomically, and splits at the first concrete conflict boundary. Split children recompute and validate queue priority, fixed-constraint facts, and local use weights.
- 2026-07-22: Final audit separated queue priority from spill weight, following the regalloc2 model. Using spill weight for both made eviction effectively unreachable because the most expensive bundle always ran first; live extent now chooses processing order while spill density decides whether displacement is profitable.
