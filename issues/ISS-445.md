## Description

AEGIS-128L spends 34,729 us in MilkIR optimization, including 20,797 us in
egraph rewriting and 5,030 us in global GVN. The optimizer time has a 0.991
correlation with surviving instruction count. ISS-438 already demonstrated the
relevant direction: replacing maps keyed by dense eclass IDs with indexed
arrays reduced egraph time by 22.2 percent without changing rewrite policy.

The remaining gap to Cranelift's optimizer is likely in hot node, edge, use,
and temporary-worklist representation rather than excessive convergence or a
single runaway rule.

## Design

Use ISS-441 attribution plus allocation/drop counters to identify the remaining
reference-counted records created per eclass, enode, parent edge, use, and
extraction step. Move only proven dense immutable facts to indexed storage or
carefully validated value representation, and reuse temporary worklists where
ownership permits.

Retain the current egraph rewrite policy, global GVN semantics, bounded memory
analysis, optimization level, and extraction result. Do not lower budgets or
disable a pass to satisfy the compile-time target.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] A source-grounded allocation/drop attribution identifies the hot optimizer records before their representation changes.
- [ ] Dense hot facts and reusable temporary collections no longer incur avoidable map, object, or reference-count traffic.
- [ ] Egraph rewrites, GVN decisions, optimized MilkIR, and generated AEGIS-128L code remain unchanged.
- [ ] Across at least seven cache-isolated AEGIS-128L captures, aggregate MilkIR optimization improves materially without moving time into lowering or regalloc.
- [ ] Function 56 remains at the 16,036-byte code-size control, and no optimization budget or memory-analysis coverage is reduced.
- [ ] MilkIR optimizer tests, formatting, warning-73 checking, and generated interfaces pass.
