## Description

Current AArch64 JIT workloads still expose potential runtime hot paths and noisy microbenchmark methodology. The retired backend is useful historical evidence but is no longer a release or migration authority.

## Design

Use stable in-module current-version benchmarks plus normalized Target VCode and disassembly to identify hot paths. Separate measurement noise from code-generation defects and improve demonstrated bottlenecks without compromising ABI, traps, GC, or metadata correctness.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Current-version runtime measurements separate in-module work from startup and JIT compilation.
- [x] Material hot-path instruction and allocation differences are identified.
- [x] High-noise workloads have stable signal or are replaced by deterministic current-version benchmarks.
- [x] Correctness, ABI, and metadata gates do not regress.

## Close Notes

Closed with the reproducible report at
`docs/perf/aarch64-jit-current-version-2026-07-23.md`. The paired,
cache-isolated seven-sample AEGIS median is `1.166985` for the guest-reported
value and `1.161701` for wall time relative to Wasmtime. This improves the
initial paired medians by 8.13% and 8.14%, respectively.

The hot `func_30` fell from 19 slots / 17 spills / 22 reloads / 1,568 bytes to
10 slots / 8 spills / 10 reloads / 1,296 bytes. Immediate shift and rotate
forms, shifted additions, pointer-offset immediates, and bounded stable-field
reuse account for the demonstrated change. The report separately records cold
compilation, cached execution, empty-module fixed overhead, raw pairs, and
generated-code metrics.

The complete native suite passed 2,307/2,307. Recursive interpreter and JIT WAST
each passed 258/258 files and 62,563 assertions. Target, regalloc, script, and
module-boundary gates also passed.
