# ISS-438: Reduce duplicate MilkIR egraph and GVN work

## Description

MilkIR optimization spends about 26 ms per AEGIS-128L module in egraph
rewriting and another 5 ms in a separate GVN traversal. Profiling traced a
material share of egraph cost to hash-map operations over dense, monotonically
allocated e-class ids.

## Design

Store dense e-class, parent-edge, type, and extraction-table data in indexed
arrays while retaining hash maps only where keys are not dense ids. Preserve
all rewrite policy and keep the bounded memory analysis from ISS-433. Do not
disable egraph globally or introduce workload-specific rules.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [x] Dense e-class ids no longer pay hash-map lookup for class, parent, type, or extraction-table storage.
- [x] Rewrite policy and bounded GVN memory analysis remain unchanged.
- [x] AEGIS-128L egraph plus GVN median time materially decreases across at least seven cold captures.
- [x] Function 56 code size remains at the recorded 16,036-byte control.
- [x] Strict native, WAST, and Component Model gates pass.

## Notes

- 2026-08-28: Current detailed metrics put egraph at 25,872 us and GVN at
  4,859 us per module. A sample recorded 11,619 eclasses, 12,376 enodes, and
  1,352 rule applications; 47 of 63 functions changed without changing their
  instruction count.

- 2026-08-28: Replaced dense-id maps for e-classes, parent edges, width facts,
  and extraction results with indexed optional arrays. Seven final cold AEGIS
  captures put egraph at 20,118 us and GVN at 4,884 us, versus 25,872 us and
  4,859 us before the change. The egraph stage improved by 22.2 percent while
  GVN remained flat, identifying dense storage rather than pass policy as the
  gain. Function 56 remains 16,036 bytes.

## Close Notes

Closed after dense storage removed the measured lookup overhead without
changing rewrites, GVN policy, or generated-code control size.
